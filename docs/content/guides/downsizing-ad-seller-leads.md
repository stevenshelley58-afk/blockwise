# How to use a downsizer campaign to uncover seller intent

Seller-lead campaigns are the most expensive and most difficult ads in real estate.

They ask directly: "Thinking of selling? Get a free home evaluation." That offer attracts two groups — people who are ready to list now (rare) and people who are curious but months away (common). The cost per lead is high because the offer signals a commitment most homeowners are not ready to make.

There is another path. It runs through a buyer ad.

## The hidden logic of a downsizer campaign

A downsizer campaign is framed as a buyer campaign. It offers a list of low-maintenance homes priced below the local average — the kind of property a downsizer would move to. The ad copy, the image and the property search on your website all look like a standard buyer-lead magnet.

But the audience it attracts has a second characteristic the ad does not mention: these people already own a more expensive home. To buy a cheaper one, they generally need to sell first.

That means a buyer lead from a downsizing ad may also represent a sale opportunity — a listing on the more expensive property and a purchase on the cheaper one. The cost per lead is comparable to a standard buyer campaign, but the downstream opportunity may include both a purchase and a sale.

This is not a trick. The ad delivers exactly what it promises: a list of homes suited to downsizers. The seller opportunity emerges naturally from the follow-up conversation, not from the ad copy.

## When the market makes this work

Downsizer campaigns perform best when market conditions push homeowners toward smaller, cheaper properties. Rising interest rates, cost-of-living pressure, empty-nest households and retirement planning all create downsizing motivation.

The signal is visible in your local data. If the average detached home sells for A$450,000 and you are seeing demand for single-storey homes in the A$300,000–A$500,000 range, the gap between "what people own" and "what people want to move to" is your downsizing audience.

If your market has no meaningful price gap between the average home and the next step down, the angle will not produce seller leads — there is no financial reason to downsize.

## Conditions to check before you launch

Five conditions determine whether a downsizer campaign will produce seller leads in your market.

1. **Price gap** — Is there a meaningful difference between the average home and the downsizer target?
2. **Housing stock** — Does your market have the property type downsizers want?
3. **Intent signal** — Are people searching for smaller homes?
4. **Feature recognition** — Can you name the property type in one phrase buyers recognise?
5. **Market timing** — Are conditions pushing people toward downsizing?

| Condition | What to check | Green light | Red light |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Price gap** | Is there a meaningful price gap between the average home and the downsizer target? | Average A$700K, target list A$500K–A$650K | Average and target are within 5% of each other |
| **2. Housing stock** | Does your market have the property type downsizers want? | Single-storey, low-maintenance, single-level villas | Only large two-storey homes available |
| **3. Intent signal** | Are people searching for or enquiring about smaller homes? | Increasing searches for "single-storey" or "downsizing" in your area | Flat or declining interest |
| **4. Feature recognition** | Can you name the downsizer property type in one phrase buyers recognise? | "Single-storey homes under A$500K" | "Various properties that might suit your needs" |
| **5. Market timing** | Are market conditions (rates, demographics, affordability) pushing people toward downsizing? | Rising rates, ageing demographic, cost pressure | Stable rates, young demographic, no cost pressure |

If three or more conditions are green, a downsizer campaign will likely produce a mix of buyer and seller leads. If two or fewer are green, choose a different angle.

## 1. Choose the price band

Find the average sale price for a detached house in your market. Set your list ceiling slightly above that average — roughly 10% higher.

Then set the floor at about A$150,000 below the ceiling. This creates a range that captures both the homes downsizers are leaving (if they are at the top of the range) and the homes they are moving to (if they are at the bottom).

For example, if the local average is A$450,000:

- Ceiling: A$500,000
- Floor: A$350,000
- Range: A$150,000

This is deliberately wider than a standard buyer-list ad. Downsizers are comparing their current home (above the range) against the target home (within the range), so the range needs to cover both sides of the decision.

## 2. Choose the feature

The feature should describe the property type a downsizer wants, not a luxury feature. Good options:

- single-storey / one-level homes
- low-maintenance properties
- properties with single-level living
- homes on smaller, manageable blocks

Avoid features that signal luxury or upgrade — pools, views, acreage. Those attract a different audience and dilute the downsizer signal.

## 3. Build the property search

Create a filtered search on your website or authorised property feed:

- Property type: detached houses
- Feature: your chosen downsizer characteristic (single-storey, low-maintenance, etc.)
- Price range: your floor to ceiling
- Sort: most popular

Copy the URL. This is where leads land after submitting the form.

Use the map view rather than the grid view if your property search supports it. Downsizers are location-sensitive — they want to see whether the smaller homes are in areas they know and like.

## 4. Set up the Meta campaign

The campaign setup mirrors a standard custom-list buyer ad, with two differences in naming and copy.

**Campaign name:** "Downsizing — [Suburb] — [Date]." The name signals the intent for your own reporting, even though the ad copy will not use the word "downsizing" prominently.

**Ad set:**

- Objective: Leads
- Conversion location: Instant forms
- Special ad category: Housing
- Budget: A$20/day for a first test
- Location: Your market centre, minimum radius per the housing special ad category
- Placements: Advantage+ or restricted to Feed + Marketplace

**Ad copy:** Frame the ad around the list, not the life event. The ad should read like a property list offer, not a downsizing service.

**Primary text:**

> Stop scrolling. The most up-to-date list of single-storey homes under A$500K in [Suburb] is here — updated daily, direct from an authorised property feed. Perfect for downsizers, first-home buyers and investors. See photos, prices and details in real time. Click below to get instant access.

**Headline:**

> [Suburb] single-storey homes under A$500K — updated daily

**Call to action:** Learn more

The word "downsizers" appears in the copy, but it is listed alongside "first-home buyers" and "investors." The ad does not single out downsizers or ask them to identify as sellers. It offers a list and lets the audience self-select.

## 5. The image

Use a photo of a single-storey home that looks like it belongs in your market at the lower end of your price range. Not a luxury property. Not a renovation showcase. A clean, ordinary home that a downsizer would look at and think, "That is manageable."

Crop to 1:1. Ensure the property is recognisable as single-storey from the thumbnail — a roofline or single-level facade should be visible.

## 6. The lead form

Use the higher-intent form with a review step. Ask for:

- Full name
- Email
- Phone number

On the thank-you screen, link to the property search and add:

> Click below to view the most popular single-storey homes under A$500K in [Suburb]. If you have questions about any of these properties or would like to view one, contact me at [phone/email].

Enable the Messenger conversation option if available.

## 7. The follow-up that finds the seller

The ad has done its job — it produced a lead interested in smaller homes. The follow-up is where you discover whether they also need to sell.

Your first call is a buyer conversation, not a listing pitch. The goal is to understand their situation:

1. **Are they already looking, or just browsing?** This tells you how warm the lead is.
2. **Do they currently own a home?** This is the key question. If yes, the next question follows naturally.
3. **Would they need to sell before buying?** Most downsizers will say yes. That is your seller lead.
4. **What is their timeline?** A shorter planning range means a warmer lead. A longer range means nurture or long-term pipeline.

When they confirm they need to sell, shift the conversation:

> "That is very common for people looking at these homes. A lot of my clients in this price range are in the same position. If it would help, I can take a look at your current property and give you a sense of what it is likely to sell for — no obligation, just so you know your numbers before you make a move."

This is not a high-pressure listing pitch. It is a practical offer tied to the buyer conversation the lead already opted into. The transition from buyer lead to seller conversation happens because the ad pre-qualified the audience, not because you tricked anyone.

## The sale and purchase opportunity

When a downsizing lead confirms they need to sell and buy, you have a chance to coordinate both the sale and the purchase. This does not happen every time — some leads already have an agent for the sale, and some will want separate representation. But the opportunity exists because you entered the relationship through the buying conversation, not by competing for a listing.

Do not assume the listing is yours. Do your buyer job well first. If you help them find the right property and build trust through the process, the listing conversation follows naturally. Forcing it on the first call will lose both transactions.

## Compliance note

Any follow-up you conduct — whether by phone, email, SMS or Messenger — must comply with Australian spam and privacy laws. You need consent to send commercial electronic messages, you must clearly identify yourself as the sender, and you must provide a functional unsubscribe facility. The ACMA provides guidance on avoiding spam: https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam

The OAIC also provides guidance on direct marketing and privacy obligations: https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing

Agency, disclosure and conflict requirements vary by state and transaction structure. If you are acting for both a buyer and a seller in a related transaction, check your state's requirements for disclosing that relationship and any conflict of interest. Some states require written disclosure of dual representation; others restrict it entirely in certain circumstances.

## How to measure this campaign

A downsizer campaign needs different metrics than a pure buyer campaign. Instead of relying on fixed benchmarks, use a campaign economics calculator to track your own data.

| Input | User enters |
|---|---|
| Monthly spend | A$ |
| Leads | Number |
| Valid contacts | Number |
| Existing homeowners | Number |
| Qualified selling conversations | Number |
| Appraisals | Number |
| Listings | Number |

Calculate rates from your own data rather than relying on fixed benchmarks. Track what percentage of your leads already own a home — your own campaign data will tell you whether the downsizing angle is working. Track how many homeowner leads need to sell before buying. Track how many seller conversations resulted in an appraisal and a listing.

The seller opportunity takes longer to surface than the buyer opportunity. A lead that buys within a shorter planning range but lists their current home with you later is a win. Judge this campaign on a 90-day horizon at minimum.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is this deceptive? The ad looks like a buyer ad but I want seller leads.

The approach is transparent when the advertised list is genuine, the properties match the stated criteria, the resource is delivered as promised, and follow-up clearly identifies the agent and purpose of contact. Agency, disclosure and conflict requirements vary by state and transaction structure — check your obligations before acting for both a buyer and a seller in a related transaction.

### What percentage of downsizing leads are actually sellers?

It varies by market and timing. Track what percentage of your leads already own a home — your own campaign data will tell you whether the downsizing angle is working. Then track how many homeowner leads need to sell before buying. Not every homeowner lead is a seller lead, but a well-targeted downsizer campaign in a market with a meaningful price gap will produce a meaningful share of leads who own and need to sell.

### Can I run this alongside a standard custom-list buyer ad?

Yes, but run them as separate campaigns with separate ad sets so you can compare performance. Do not combine downsizing and general buyer targeting in one ad set — you will not be able to tell which angle is producing the seller leads.

### How long before I see seller results from this campaign?

Results vary by market and individual circumstances. Buyer closings may happen within a shorter planning range. Seller listings from the same campaign typically take longer because the homeowner needs to resolve their buying decision before committing to a sale. Track this campaign on a 90-day horizon at minimum.

### What if my market does not have a meaningful price gap?

If the average home and the downsizer target are within 5% of each other, there is no financial reason for homeowners to downsize. In that case, choose a different angle — a feature-based list (homes with pools, corner blocks) or a first-home-buyer list — rather than forcing a downsizing angle that the market does not support.
