# How to use the Meta Ad Library without copying competitors

The [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library) lets anyone view advertisements that Pages are currently running across Meta's products.

That makes it useful for local real estate research. It does not make it a performance database.

For ordinary commercial advertisements, seeing an ad in the library does not tell you how much the agent is spending, how many leads it generated, whether those leads were qualified, whether the ad produced a listing or whether it has been profitable.

An active advertisement is evidence that the ad exists. It is not proof that the ad works.

## The SCAN framework

Use four steps to turn Ad Library research into actionable advertising decisions.

| Step | What to do | What you learn | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S: Survey the market** | Search for local agents and record every active ad | What the local message environment looks like | Only checking one competitor |
| **C: Classify the patterns** | Sort ads by offer, situation, proof and format | Where concentration and gaps exist | Noting "nice creative" without recording the proposition |
| **A: Assess the gaps** | Identify which seller situations nobody is addressing | Where you can differentiate | Copying a competitor's format instead of finding a missing concept |
| **N: Navigate to original ads** | Build ads from your own evidence that fill the gaps | A differentiated creative portfolio | Borrowing a competitor's script or visual arrangement |

If you only do step S, you have a snapshot. If you do S and C but not A and N, you have a map with no plan.

## 1. Survey the market

Open the [Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library) and search for local agents by name or Page. The library shows all active ads running across Meta technologies, including Facebook and Instagram.

Record what you see for each agent:

| Field | What to note |
|---|---|
| Suburb mentioned | Does the ad name a specific area? |
| Offer | What does the person receive? |
| Audience situation | Which type of homeowner is the message addressing? |
| Property type | Is a specific property type named or shown? |
| Format | Static image, carousel, video, Reel |
| Agent on camera | Does the agent appear, or is it text and graphics? |
| Proof | What evidence supports the claim? |
| Call to action | What is the next step? |
| Duration | Has the ad been visible across several reviews? |

Do this for every competitor you can find in your service area. A single review provides a snapshot. Repeated reviews — monthly, for example — reveal movement: new offers entering the market, ads that disappear quickly, concepts that remain active, and suburbs receiving more attention.

### Longevity is not proof

An ad that has been running for months may be profitable. It may also be an ad the agent forgot to turn off. Longevity is a signal worth noting, not a performance metric. The Ad Library does not show spend, impressions, click-through rate or lead volume.

## 2. Classify the patterns

Once you have surveyed several agents, sort their ads into four categories.

### Offer

What does the person receive?

Common real estate offers include:

- Free appraisal
- Market report
- Just listed notification
- Just sold notification
- Strategy call
- Property review

### Audience situation

Which type of homeowner is the message addressing?

- Active seller comparing agents
- Curious owner checking the market
- Preparing seller planning ahead
- Buyer (not relevant for seller-lead strategy, but note if competitors are spending on buyer ads)

### Proof

Why should the person believe the claim?

- Sales count
- Recent comparable sales
- Testimonial
- Case study
- No proof provided

### Format

How is the message delivered?

- Static image
- Carousel
- Agent-led video
- Client-led video
- Property footage

### Build a local advertising map

| Agent | Offer | Situation | Proof | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agent A | Appraisal | Active seller | Sales count | Static |
| Agent B | Market report | Curious owner | Recent sales | Carousel |
| Agent C | Strategy call | Preparing seller | Testimonial | Video |
| Agent D | Just sold | Curious owner | Recent sale | Static |
| Agent E | Appraisal | Active seller | No proof | Static |

Once several agents are mapped, the local sameness becomes obvious. In most markets, the table fills up with one or two offers repeated across nearly every agent.

## 3. Assess the gaps

Suppose nearly every local agent is running one of these messages:

- Free appraisal
- Just listed
- Just sold
- Meet your local agent
- We have buyers waiting

That concentration creates an opportunity.

The answer is not necessarily to make a more attractive appraisal graphic. It may be to choose a different proposition entirely:

- Recent sales by property type (not just "just sold" — a structured comparable)
- Buyer-demand changes (what buyers are paying more for)
- Pre-sale preparation (renovation, timing, costs)
- Selling-cost clarity (what it actually costs to sell)
- Renovation decisions (renovate vs sell as-is)
- A case study explaining one result in detail

The gap may be conceptual rather than visual. If every competitor is running appraisal ads and nobody is running a sold-price report, a renovation guide or a buyer-demand update, those are open propositions.

### The three-question test

A useful competitor process asks:

1. What is everyone saying?
2. What useful seller problem is being ignored?
3. What evidence can we provide that competitors cannot?

The third question is the most important. An agent's strongest material usually comes from their own market activity:

- Real buyer questions from open homes
- Real appraisal objections from vendor conversations
- Real sales patterns from recent transactions
- Real negotiation examples from closed deals
- Real client experiences from past listings

Competitor research should direct attention back to that material — not become a template collection exercise.

## 4. Navigate to original ads

The goal is not to look more like the other agents advertising nearby. It is to give homeowners a better reason to stop.

### Extract the principle, not the asset

A competitor's repeated use of client video may indicate that testimonials are strategically important. That does not justify copying the script, visual arrangement or wording.

Extract the principle:

> Local seller proof deserves a place in our portfolio.

Then produce original evidence from your own business.

The same applies to:

- Carousels — learn from the format, not the execution
- Agent-led video — the principle is familiarity, not the same script
- Sold-property maps — the principle is local evidence, not the same map
- Market reports — the principle is data, not the same chart
- Lead forms — the principle is low friction, not the same questions

### Build from your own evidence

Use the gaps you identified to create ads that competitors are not running:

| Gap identified | Ad concept | Your evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody addresses renovation decisions | "Renovate before selling, or leave it alone?" | Your experience with renovated vs unrenovated sales |
| Nobody shows buyer-demand data | "What Como buyers are paying more for" | Your buyer enquiry and open-home data |
| Nobody explains selling costs | "What it actually costs to sell a home in [Suburb]" | Your fee structure and typical marketing costs |
| Nobody uses client video | A seller describing their experience | Your past clients who have consented to be featured |

Each ad should be built from evidence you can support. A local claim should not be invented merely to create a stronger hook.

## 5. Review regularly

A single Ad Library review provides a snapshot. Repeated reviews reveal the direction of the market.

Set a monthly calendar reminder and track:

- New offers entering the market — did a competitor start running a renovation guide?
- Ads that disappear quickly — was that a test that failed, or a budget that ran out?
- Concepts that remain active — what is durable?
- Seasonal changes — do appraisal ads increase in spring?
- Suburbs receiving more attention — is a competitor expanding into your farm area?
- Agents increasing creative variety — is someone building a portfolio?

Record each review in a simple document. Over three to six months, patterns will emerge that a single snapshot cannot reveal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I see how much my competitors are spending on Meta ads?

No. The Ad Library shows active ads and some basic information, but it does not display spend, impressions, click-through rates or lead volume for ordinary commercial advertisements. An active ad means the ad exists — not that it is performing well.

### Is it legal to look at competitor ads in the Ad Library?

Yes. The Meta Ad Library is a public tool. Viewing ads that Pages are running is its intended purpose. The issue is not viewing — it is copying. Extract the principle behind a competitor's approach, then produce original ads from your own evidence.

### How often should I check the Ad Library?

Monthly is a practical cadence for most local agents. Set a calendar reminder, record what you see, and compare against previous reviews. Patterns emerge over three to six months that a single snapshot cannot reveal.

### What if every competitor is already running the same offers I planned to run?

That is the most useful finding you can make. If everyone is running appraisal ads and sold-price reports, look for the next layer: renovation guides, selling-cost explanations, buyer-demand updates, or client case studies. The goal is not to match the market — it is to find the proposition nobody else is offering.
