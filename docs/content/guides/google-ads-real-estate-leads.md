# Google Ads for real estate: how to close 2–3 deals a month on a small budget

Facebook ads are the easiest way to generate real estate leads. Google Ads are the easiest way to generate real estate leads that actually close.

The difference is intent. A Facebook user was scrolling through their feed when your ad appeared — they were not searching for a home. A Google user typed "homes for sale in [city]" into a search bar. They are actively looking. That intent means Google leads cost more per click, but they convert at a higher rate because the person is closer to the purchase point.

This guide covers the full setup: keyword strategy, ad copy, landing page design, budget guidance, and the follow-up system that turns those clicks into closed transactions.

## Facebook vs. Google: which to use

Most agents should not choose one over the other — they serve different purposes.

| Factor | Facebook Ads | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead intent** | Low to medium — interruptive, the person was not searching | High — the person actively searched for property |
| **Cost per lead** | Lower ($4–$12 typical) | Higher ($13–$30 typical for real estate) |
| **Lead volume** | Higher — larger audience, lower friction | Lower — limited by search volume in your market |
| **Lead quality** | Variable — many early-stage browsers | Stronger — closer to purchase decision |
| **Budget to start** | $10–$20/day | $15–$20/day |
| **Time to first lead** | Within 24 hours | 1–2 weeks (algorithm needs time) |
| **Best for** | Building a pipeline of future buyers | Generating leads ready to talk now |

If you can only run one, start with Facebook if you have strong follow-up systems, or Google if you are weak at nurturing and need higher-intent leads to compensate. If you can run both, use Facebook for volume and Google for quality, and compare the cost-per-closed-deal over 90 days rather than the cost-per-lead over 14 days.

## The SCALE framework

Five decisions determine whether your Google Ads campaign will produce closable leads.

| Decision | What to choose | Example | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S: Search terms** | Keywords people actually type when looking for homes in your market | "homes for sale in Winston Salem" | "real estate agent near me" (too generic, attracts job seekers and other agents) |
| **C: City focus** | One city or region, not a whole state | "new construction homes Winston Salem NC" | "North Carolina real estate" (too broad, low relevance) |
| **A: Ad specificity** | Headlines and descriptions that match the keyword and promise a specific result | "New Construction Homes in Winston Salem — 250+ Listings, Free Sign Up" | "Find your dream home today" (generic, no specific promise) |
| **L: Landing page match** | The page the ad links to matches exactly what the ad promised | Ad says "new construction $300K–$400K" → page shows only new construction in that range | Ad says "new construction" → page shows all property types and price ranges |
| **E: Estimated budget** | Enough to generate data, not so much you waste money on an untested campaign | $15–$20/day for a first test | $5/day (too little for data) or $100/day (too much for a first test) |

If any of these are weak, the campaign will struggle regardless of how well you optimise other elements.

## 1. Keyword strategy

Google Ads for real estate is search-based — you bid on keywords that buyers type into Google. The most effective keywords are specific to your market and property type.

**Core keywords:**

- `homes for sale in [city]`
- `houses for sale in [city]`
- `[city] real estate`
- `new construction homes [city]`
- `[neighbourhood] homes for sale`

**Refined keywords (higher intent):**

- `new construction homes [city] [price range]`
- `[property type] for sale in [city]` (e.g., "townhouses for sale in [city]")
- `homes for sale in [city] under [price]`

**Negative keywords (exclude these):**

Add these as negative keywords to prevent your ad showing for irrelevant searches:

- `jobs`, `careers`, `hiring`
- `rent`, `rental`, `lease`
- `commercial`, `office`, `retail`
- `cheap`, `foreclosure`, `auction` (unless you specifically target these)

Check what competitors are bidding on by searching Google for your core keywords. Look at the ads appearing at the top of the results — the headlines and descriptions they use tell you what is working in your market. Do not copy them, but note the patterns: price points mentioned, specific neighbourhoods named, free sign-up offers.

## 2. Write the ad

A Google search ad has three components: headlines, descriptions and a display URL.

**Headlines (up to 30 characters each):**

Write 3–5 headlines. Google rotates them and shows the best-performing combination. Include your keyword in at least one headline.

1. `Homes for Sale in [City]` (matches the search term directly)
2. `New Construction $300K–$400K` (specific price point)
3. `250+ Listings — Free Sign Up` (volume + free offer)
4. `See Photos, Prices & Details` (what they get)
5. `[City] Real Estate Made Easy` (emotional benefit)

**Descriptions (up to 90 characters each):**

1. `Browse new construction homes in [City], NC. Updated daily. Free to search — no obligation.`
2. `250+ active listings in [City]. Filter by price, neighbourhood and property type. Start now.`

**Call to action:** Do not waste description text on "click here" — Google handles the click. Use the description to add specific detail the headlines cannot fit.

The principle is simple: **make the ad match the search.** If someone searches "new construction homes Winston Salem," the ad should say "New Construction Homes in Winston Salem" — not "Find Your Dream Home." Specificity beats cleverness.

## 3. Build the landing page

The landing page is where most Google Ads campaigns fail. If the ad promises new construction homes in a specific price range and the landing page shows a random mix of properties, the visitor bounces.

**The match rule:** The landing page must show exactly what the ad promised. If the ad says "new construction $300K–$400K in [city]," the page should show a list of new construction homes in that city, in that price range — nothing else.

**Format: List view, not map view.** A list view is easier to scan. A map view requires the visitor to hover, click and explore to find properties, which adds friction. Give them the list first; they can switch to map view if they want.

**Lead capture: Force registration after 3 property views.** Let the visitor browse a few properties and photos to establish interest, then require registration (name, email, phone) to continue viewing. This is the standard IDX lead capture pattern.

Ask for all three fields: **name, email and phone number.** A lead without a phone number is an email subscriber, not a lead. Deals happen on the phone, and you cannot call an email address.

Do not offer social login (Google sign-in, Facebook sign-in) as the only registration option. These typically capture only name and email, not phone number. If you offer social login, also require a phone number field.

## 4. Set the budget

Start at $15–$20 per day. This produces enough data to evaluate performance without risking significant spend on an untested campaign.

At $15–$20/day, expect:

- **Weeks 1–2:** High cost per lead ($25–$30). The algorithm is learning your audience.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Cost per lead drops ($15–$20). The algorithm has optimised delivery.
- **Weeks 5+:** Cost per lead stabilises ($10–$15 in many markets). You can scale budget.

Do not expect results in the first week. Google Ads, like Facebook, needs time for the algorithm to identify which users are most likely to convert. Turning the ad off after a week because leads are expensive is the same mistake as turning off a Facebook ad after two days.

### The ROI calculation that changes your mindset

If your average commission is $8,000 and you spend $2,000 on ads to close one deal, your return is 4x. That is not an expense — it is an investment with a measurable return.

The agents who fail with Google Ads are the ones who spend $500, see no deals in the first month, and quit. The agents who succeed commit to a 90-day test, learn the platform, and let the data tell them whether to continue, adjust or stop.

## 5. Monitor and adjust

The key metric for judging a Google Ads campaign is **click-through rate (CTR)**. The industry standard for search ads is 3–5%.

- **CTR above 5%:** Your ad is compelling and relevant. The keyword-to-ad-to-landing-page chain is working. Consider increasing budget.
- **CTR between 3–5%:** The ad is performing adequately. Look for small improvements in headline copy or landing page load speed.
- **CTR below 3%:** The ad is not compelling enough. Revise headlines to be more specific to the keyword. Check whether the landing page matches the ad promise.

If CTR is dropping over time, refresh the headlines. Ad fatigue is real — the same headlines shown to the same audience for weeks will see declining performance. Rotate new headlines in every 2–3 weeks.

### When to turn an ad off

Turn the ad off when:

- CTR has been below 2% for two weeks despite headline revisions
- The landing page bounce rate is above 80% (the ad-to-page match is broken)
- Cost per lead has not improved after 30 days and is above $30
- You have not generated a single conversation-ready lead in 60 days

Do not turn the ad off because:

- You have not closed a deal in the first month (deals take 30–90 days from lead to close)
- One week had high spend and few leads (weekly variance is normal)
- You feel like you are spending too much (calculate ROI, not absolute spend)

## 6. The follow-up system

Google Ads leads are higher-intent than Facebook leads, but they still require systematic follow-up. The same cadence applies — call within 24 hours, then stay on a rotation — with two adjustments.

**Faster first call.** Because Google leads cost more and are higher-intent, prioritise them in your call queue. If you have 10 Facebook leads and 3 Google leads, call the Google leads first.

**Shorter nurture phase.** Google leads tend to have shorter timelines because they were actively searching. A Google lead that does not convert in 30 days is less likely to convert than a Facebook lead at the same point. Do not abandon them, but do not invest the same nurturing effort — the 21-day rotation may be more efficient than weekly calls for Google leads who did not convert in the first two weeks.

## The realistic timeline to 2–3 deals per month

This is not an overnight system. Based on the data from agents running this approach:

| Timeframe | What to expect |
|---|---|
| **Month 1** | Campaign setup, algorithm learning. High cost per lead. 1–2 leads per day. No deals yet. |
| **Month 2** | Cost per lead drops. Lead quality improves. First conversations and appointments. Possibly first closing. |
| **Month 3** | Steady lead flow. Appointments converting to contracts. 1–2 deals this month. |
| **Month 4–6** | Follow-up pipeline mature. 2–3 deals per month becomes realistic. Budget can scale. |
| **Month 6+** | Peak months can produce 4–7 deals. Consistent 2–3 per month is sustainable. |

The agents who reach 2–3 deals per month are the ones who commit to the 90-day test, run the follow-up cadence consistently, and resist the urge to turn the ad off when results are not immediate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should I spend on Google Ads for real estate?

Start at $15–$20 per day for a first test. This produces enough data to evaluate the campaign within 2–3 weeks. Scale to $50–$60 per day once the campaign is producing leads at a sustainable cost per lead (typically $10–$15 in most markets). Do not start at $5/day — the budget is too low for the algorithm to learn.

### Are Google Ads better than Facebook Ads for real estate?

Neither is universally better. Google produces higher-intent leads because the user actively searched for property. Facebook produces more leads at a lower cost per lead. Use both if budget allows — Facebook for pipeline volume, Google for ready-to-talk leads. Compare them on cost-per-closed-deal, not cost-per-lead.

### What website platform should I use for the landing page?

Real Geeks, Bold Trail, KV Core and Lofty all support IDX landing pages with forced registration. The platform matters less than the match between the ad and the page. Whatever you use, ensure the page shows properties that match the ad's promise and captures name, email and phone number.

### How long does it take for Google Ads to start working?

Expect 2–3 weeks of high cost per lead while the algorithm learns. By week 4, cost per lead should stabilise. By month 2, you should be having conversations and setting appointments. By month 3, you should be closing your first deals from the campaign. Agents who quit in the first month never see these results.

### Should I use Google's Performance Max or manual search campaigns?

For a first real estate campaign, manual search campaigns give you more control over keywords, ad copy and budget allocation. Performance Max uses Google's AI to optimise across all channels, but it is less transparent and harder to diagnose when performance is poor. Start with manual search, then experiment with Performance Max once you understand what works.
