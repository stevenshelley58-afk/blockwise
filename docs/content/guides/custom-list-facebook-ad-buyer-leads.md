# The custom-list Facebook ad that generates buyer leads in 24 hours

Most real estate Facebook ads fail for one of two reasons: the offer is too generic, or the setup is too complicated.

"Check out my listings" is not an offer. It does not give a scrolling buyer a reason to stop, and it does not give Meta's algorithm enough signal to find the right people. A generic brand-awareness campaign burns budget without producing contactable leads.

The ad that works reliably is simpler than most agents expect. It offers one thing: a custom list of local homes, filtered by a price point that casts the widest net, with one desirable feature that makes the list feel curated rather than random. The ad runs as a Meta instant form, asks for name, email and phone, and delivers the list immediately.

This guide walks through the full setup — from choosing your price point and feature to publishing the ad and connecting your CRM.

## What makes the offer work

The offer is a filtered IDX list. Not a landing page. Not a market report. Not a "free home valuation." A specific, current list of homes that match criteria a buyer would actually search for.

Two decisions make or break it.

**Price point.** Find the average sale price for a single-family detached home in your market, then set your filter slightly above it. If the average is $475,000, use $500,000 as your ceiling. That captures the widest pool of buyers: first-home buyers, move-up buyers, downsizers and relocators. A ceiling that is too low excludes the bulk of the market; one that is too high feels out of reach for the people most likely to respond.

**Desirable feature.** Pick one feature that is in high demand in your market. Pools in a hot climate. Single-storey homes in an area where downsizers are active. Corner blocks. Walk-out basements. Properties with views. The feature should be something buyers actively search for and can immediately recognise in the ad copy.

The combination — a price ceiling plus one feature — is what makes the list feel curated. "Homes under $500,000 with a pool" is a list someone wants. "Homes in Phoenix" is not.

## The SETUP checklist

Before you open Ads Manager, work through these five decisions.

| Step | What to decide | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **S: Suburb and radius** | Your market centre, 15-mile minimum radius (housing ad category requirement) | Defines the audience the algorithm will optimise within |
| **E: Exact price ceiling** | Average detached price + ~5–10%, rounded to a clean number | Casts the widest net without being meaningless |
| **T: Target feature** | One highly desirable property characteristic for your market | Makes the list feel curated and qualifies the response |
| **U: URL for the list** | A filtered IDX search link matching your price and feature criteria | The thank-you screen sends leads here immediately |
| **P: Privacy policy URL** | A live privacy policy page on your website | Meta requires this to publish a lead form |

If any of these are missing, finish them first. The ad will not publish without a privacy policy, and a broken IDX link will waste every lead the campaign generates.

## 1. Build the list on your IDX site

Before touching Ads Manager, create the filtered search on your IDX website.

1. Navigate to your IDX search and select **single-family** as the property type.
2. Apply your feature filter — for example, one-storey homes, homes with pools, or corner blocks.
3. Set the maximum price to your chosen ceiling.
4. Set the minimum price to roughly $150,000 below the ceiling. This creates a meaningful range without narrowing the audience too aggressively.
5. Sort by **most popular** so the list shows the homes generating the most engagement.
6. Copy the URL. This is where your leads will land after submitting the form.

The list should look current and complete. If the results are thin, widen the price range or choose a different feature before proceeding — do not publish an ad that sends people to an empty search page.

## 2. Create the campaign

Open Meta Ads Manager and create a new campaign.

- **Objective:** Leads
- **Campaign type:** Manual leads campaign (not the streamlined/tailored recommendation). Manual gives you control over budget, targeting and creative — the streamlined option removes decisions you need to make.
- **Special Ad Category:** Housing. This is mandatory for any real estate ad. It restricts age, gender and detailed targeting, but the algorithm compensates by finding the right audience based on engagement signals.
- **Campaign name:** Use a descriptive name like "Custom list under 500K — [Suburb] — [Date]." You will reuse this name at the ad set and ad levels with slight variations. Naming matters because six months from now you need to know which campaign produced which leads.

## 3. Configure the ad set

The ad set controls budget, schedule, audience and placements.

**Conversion location:** Instant forms. This keeps the lead on Facebook or Instagram rather than sending them to an external page, which reduces friction and improves form completion.

**Facebook page:** Select your business page. If you have not accepted the Meta lead ads terms of service, you will be prompted to do so before proceeding.

**Budget:** Start at $20 a day. This is enough to collect meaningful data without overspending on an untested ad. A $10 budget works but takes longer to produce enough data to judge performance. At $20 a day, you will spend no more than $140 per week — Meta averages spend across the week, so individual days may vary by up to 25% above or below the daily figure.

**Schedule:** Set a start date (the next morning is fine) and an end date two to three weeks out. An end date prevents the most common waste pattern: an agent forgets about the ad, it runs for three months, and nobody follows up with the leads. You can always extend a successful ad.

**Location:** Select your city. The housing special ad category enforces a 15-mile minimum radius. That covers most market centres adequately. Do not try to narrow further — the restriction exists and the algorithm works within it.

**Detailed targeting:** Leave it broad. The housing category limits detailed targeting significantly, and the algorithm is better at finding the right audience through engagement signals than you are through interest stacking. If you have the option, targeting people interested in property portals (Zillow, Realtor.com, realestate.com.au) is a reasonable starting point, but do not overthink it.

**Placements:** Advantage+ (automatic) placements is the simplest option for a first test. If you want more control, restrict to Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed and Facebook Marketplace — remove Audience Network, Stories and Reels for a single-image ad.

## 4. Write the ad copy with AI

Use a large-language-model tool (ChatGPT, Claude or similar) to draft your primary text and headline. Give it specific inputs:

> I am a residential real estate agent in [city]. I need the highest-converting Facebook ad for homes under [price] with [specific feature]. I want copy that grabs attention, states that this list is updated, and includes a headline and ad copy.

Review the output and make two checks:

1. **Accuracy.** If the AI claims something that is not true about your market or your list, fix it. Common issues include claiming a portal does not have certain listings when it does, or implying the list is exclusive when it is a public IDX search.
2. **First three lines.** Only the first three lines of primary text are visible before a user must tap "see more." Put the strongest hook in those lines. Do not waste them on spacing or a slow build.

The call to action that converts highest is **Learn more**. Not "Sign up" — that signals a registration barrier. "Learn more" gets more clicks, and the instant form handles the registration.

## 5. Choose the right image

Use a single static image, not a video.

A static image of a property that looks like it belongs in your market at your price point with your chosen feature. If you are advertising homes under $500,000 with pools, the image should show a home that a local buyer would recognise as a $500,000 home with a pool — not a luxury estate that sets the wrong expectation.

Crop to 1:1 (square). This format takes up the most screen space in Feed and gives the ad the largest visual footprint. Avoid leaving wasted space above or below the image.

Use an image you have the rights to: your own listing photo, a photo you have permission to use, or a stock image that matches the criteria. Do not pull images from portal listings without usage rights.

## 6. Design the instant form

The form is where leads are captured. Two settings matter most.

**Higher intent vs. more volume.** Choose higher intent. This adds a review step where the person confirms their contact details before submitting. You will get slightly fewer leads, but they will be higher quality — people who confirm their email and phone are giving you the details they actually use, not the ones Facebook auto-populated from a 10-year-old account.

**Questions.** Ask for three fields only:

- Full name
- Email address
- Phone number

Do not add multiple-choice questions, qualifying surveys or optional fields. Every additional field reduces completion rate. If you need more qualification, do it in the follow-up call.

**Privacy policy.** Paste the URL of your website's privacy policy. Meta will not publish the form without one. If your site does not have a privacy policy page, create one before proceeding.

**Thank-you screen.** Set the thank-you message to direct people to your IDX list immediately:

> Click below to view the most popular [feature] homes under [price] in [suburb].

Link to the filtered IDX search URL you created in Step 1. Also include your contact information and an invitation to reach out with questions — every channel you offer is another way a lead can contact you directly.

**Messenger conversation.** If available in your account, enable the option to start a conversation in Messenger after the form is submitted. This opens a direct chat with the lead immediately, which is especially useful if you are slow to call or the lead prefers messaging.

## 7. Connect your CRM

Leads sitting in Ads Manager that nobody sees are wasted money.

Meta now supports native CRM connections for several platforms, including Follow Up Boss, KV Core and Lofty. Search for your CRM in the lead delivery settings, connect your account, and leads will flow through automatically.

If your CRM does not have a native connection, use Zapier to route leads from the Meta form to your CRM, email or Google Sheet. The specific tool matters less than the outcome: every lead must arrive somewhere you will see it and act on it within hours, not days.

## The three-day rule

When the ad goes live, do not expect optimal results on day one.

Meta's algorithm needs roughly three days to identify which users engage with your ad and optimise delivery toward similar people. You will likely see leads within the first 24 hours, but performance typically improves after the three-day learning period.

If the ad is not performing after three days, check these things before turning it off:

- Is the image relevant to the price point and feature?
- Does the first three lines of copy include a clear hook?
- Is the IDX link working and showing results?
- Is the form asking for too much information?

Make one change at a time, then give it another three days. Turning an ad off after 48 hours because it has not produced 20 leads is the most common way agents conclude that "Facebook ads don't work" — when the ad simply did not have enough time to find its audience.

## How to read the results

After the test window, review these measures:

| Measure | What it tells you | Healthy signal |
|---|---|---|
| Lead volume | Whether the budget and audience are producing form submissions | 3–14 leads per day at $20/day is a reasonable range for a local housing ad |
| Cost per lead | What Meta charges per submitted form | Varies by market; compare against your commission per deal, not against a universal benchmark |
| Contactable rate | Whether the phone and email work | Above 60% suggests the higher-intent form is doing its job |
| Speed to first contact | How quickly you respond | Same-day response is the minimum standard; under five minutes is the goal |
| Conversation rate | How many leads enter a property discussion | The true measure of whether the offer and follow-up are aligned |

Cost per lead in isolation is misleading. A $4 lead that never answers the phone costs more in time than a $12 lead that picks up on the second call.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why a single image instead of a video or carousel?

Single-image ads consistently produce the lowest cost per lead and the highest click-through rate for this type of offer. People scrolling through Feed decide in a fraction of a second whether to engage — a clear image communicates the offer faster than a video they have to watch. Carousels can work but require careful property selection and are harder to get right on a first test.

### Do I need the special ad category for housing?

Yes. Any Meta ad that relates to housing — including property listings, lead generation for real estate services, and home valuation offers — must use the Housing special ad category. Running a housing ad without it risks the ad being disapproved or your ad account being restricted.

### Can I target specific neighbourhoods or postcodes?

The housing special ad category prevents targeting below a 15-mile radius and removes postcode-level targeting. This is a platform-wide restriction for housing ads, not a setting you can override. The algorithm optimises within the allowed radius based on who engages with your ad.

### What if my IDX list has very few results?

Widen the price range or choose a different feature before publishing the ad. Sending leads to an empty or near-empty search page breaks the promise made by the ad and wastes your budget. A list of 15+ properties is a reasonable minimum for a two-week test.

### Should I use a daily budget or a lifetime budget?

A daily budget is simpler for a first test. Meta averages spend across the week, so you may spend $25 on a strong day and $15 on a slower day, but weekly spend will not exceed seven times your daily budget. Use a lifetime budget only if you need a hard cap on total spend.
