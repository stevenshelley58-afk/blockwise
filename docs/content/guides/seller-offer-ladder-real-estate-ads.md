# Stop showing every homeowner the same appraisal ad

The appraisal ad is not dead. It is being asked to do too much.

Most homeowners who see a real estate advertisement are not ready to invite an agent into their home. Some are curious. Some are watching the market. Some are planning a move next year. Others have only recently started discussing the possibility of selling.

A direct appraisal request is appropriate for a small part of that audience. Showing it to everyone forces homeowners to choose between making a high-commitment enquiry and doing nothing. Most choose nothing.

## The commitment gap

Consider what an appraisal form can imply to a homeowner:

- An agent will call me
- I may be asked when I am selling
- I will have to provide my address and phone number
- This may begin a sales process I am not ready to start

The offer may be free, but it does not feel frictionless.

A better advertising system provides several entry points — each with a level of commitment that matches where the homeowner actually is.

## The STEP framework

Use four levels to build an offer ladder that covers the full range of homeowner intent.

| Level | What it offers | Commitment required | Example | Who it attracts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S: Surface** | Local information with no personal disclosure | Name and email | Recent sold-price list for a specific property type | The curious owner checking the market |
| **T: Transition** | Property-specific education for early decision-making | Name, email, property type | Renovate-or-sell decision guide | The owner beginning to plan |
| **E: Engage** | Individual advice involving a conversation | Name, email, phone, property address | Desktop property review or pricing strategy call | The owner ready to speak with an agent |
| **P: Purchase** | A formal appointment tied to a listing decision | Full contact details, preferred times | In-person appraisal or selling consultation | The active seller comparing agents |

If every ad in your account sits at level P, you are asking the entire market to skip three steps.

## 1. Level S — Surface: local information

These offers help homeowners understand their immediate market without pretending they are ready to list:

- Recent sold-price list for a specific property type in one suburb
- Quarterly suburb market snapshot
- Current buyer-demand update
- Guide to properties selling above or below expectations

The owner receives useful evidence without declaring an intention to sell. The commitment is low: a name and an email address.

### Why this works

A suburb market report is broad. A list of recent sales for "single-storey homes on full blocks in Dianella" feels personal to someone who owns exactly that kind of property. The owner can tell in a second whether the list is relevant to their home.

This is not a shortcut to an immediate listing. It is an early-intent campaign. Its job is to identify local owners who care enough about recent comparable sales to ask for the detail.

### Form design

Ask only for information you will use:

- Full name
- Email address
- One qualifying question, such as "Do you own a home of this type in [Suburb]?"

Link to the agency [privacy policy](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing) and explain what happens after submission. If you plan to send monthly updates, ask for express consent in plain language.

Real estate campaigns on Meta require selecting the [Special Ad Category: Housing](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1198401317374558). This applies regardless of which offer level you are running — a sold-price list is still a housing-related ad.

## 2. Level T — Transition: property-specific education

These offers help the owner make an early decision. They are beginning to consider action but may not be comparing agents yet:

- Renovate or sell-as-is assessment
- Pre-sale preparation checklist
- Guide to the costs of selling
- Timeline from appraisal to settlement

The commitment is slightly higher: the owner is acknowledging they are thinking about selling, even if the timeline is uncertain.

### How this differs from surface-level offers

A sold-price list answers "What is happening in my area?" A renovation guide answers "What should I do about it?" The second question implies more intent, and the form can ask for slightly more information — property type, for example — because the offer is property-specific.

### Follow-up approach

Someone who requested a pre-sale checklist should not receive a call that begins with "When are you selling?" A better opening continues the reason they engaged:

> You downloaded the pre-sale preparation guide. Was there a particular part of the process you were working through — timing, presentation, or costs?

That question continues the conversation the ad started. It does not jump ahead.

## 3. Level E — Engage: individual advice

These offers require more involvement. The owner now has a reason to speak with an agent:

- Desktop property review (a written assessment based on available data, not a visit)
- Pricing strategy call (15 minutes by phone)
- Suburb-specific sales analysis for their property type

The commitment is meaningfully higher: the owner is providing a phone number and agreeing to a conversation. But it is still not an appraisal — there is no home visit, no expectation of a listing presentation, and no pressure to sign an agency agreement.

### Why this level matters

This is the bridge between passive interest and active selling. Some owners will never move from Level S directly to Level P — the jump is too large. Level E gives them a reason to talk to you first, which builds trust and gives you the opportunity to earn the appraisal.

### Form design

At this level, ask for:

- Full name
- Email address
- Mobile number
- Property address (for a desktop review)
- A timeframe question: "Are you considering a move in the next 3, 6, or 12 months?"

Use the [higher-intent form option](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/ad-objectives/lead-generation/lead-ads-with-forms) if available in your account, so the person reviews their details before submitting. Include SMS verification to reduce fake numbers.

## 4. Level P — Purchase: the appraisal

The appraisal remains the highest-commitment offer. It is appropriate for the active seller — the person who is comparing agents and ready to have someone assess their property in person:

- In-person appraisal
- Full selling consultation
- Comparative market analysis with a home visit

This is the offer most agents run exclusively. It should be one of several, not the only one.

### When to use it

The appraisal ad is strongest when it is surrounded by lower-commitment offers. A homeowner who downloaded a sold-price list three months ago, received a renovation guide last month, and is now seeing an appraisal ad has been warmed by the previous offers. The appraisal ad appearing cold to someone who has never heard of you is the hardest version to make work.

## 5. A practical example: four ads for one suburb

Assume an agent wants more seller opportunities in Como.

| Ad | Level | Headline | Offer | Form fields |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Surface | What three-bedroom Como homes sold for this quarter | Download the recent sales list | Name, email |
| 2 | Transition | Renovate before selling, or leave it alone? | Get the pre-sale decision guide | Name, email, property type |
| 3 | Engage | Planning to sell in Como this year? | Request a 15-minute pricing strategy call | Name, email, phone, timeframe |
| 4 | Purchase | Ready for an in-person appraisal? | Book a property consultation | Name, email, phone, address, preferred times |

These ads support the same commercial objective. They simply approach it through different homeowner situations and different commitment levels.

## 6. Do not judge every offer by immediate listings

A sold-price download may not produce a listing agreement this week. That does not make it worthless.

The correct question is whether people entering through that offer later become:

- Contactable prospects
- Engaged database contacts
- Appraisal appointments
- Active sellers
- Signed listings

This requires follow-up and measurement. Without it, agents often turn off useful early-stage offers because the immediate cost per lead appears worse than expected or because the leads are not ready when first called.

## 7. Match the follow-up to the offer

Each offer should begin a relevant conversation.

| Offer level | Wrong follow-up | Right follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Surface (sold-price list) | "When are you selling?" | "Was there a particular property type or result you were comparing?" |
| Transition (renovation guide) | "Can I come out and appraise your home?" | "Which part of the guide were you working through — timing, presentation, or costs?" |
| Engage (strategy call) | Sending a generic listing presentation | Discussing their property type, local comparable sales and their timeline |
| Purchase (appraisal) | Rescheduling or slow confirmation | Confirming the appointment and explaining what to expect |

The advertising and follow-up should feel like one process, not two unrelated systems.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I stop running appraisal ads entirely?

No. The appraisal is the top of the ladder — it belongs there. The problem is running it as the only offer. Keep the appraisal ad, but surround it with lower-commitment offers that capture homeowners who are not ready for that step yet.

### How many offer levels should I start with?

Start with two: a surface-level offer (like a sold-price list) and the appraisal. Once those are running and you have follow-up in place, add a transition-level offer. The full four-level ladder is the goal, but you do not need to build it all at once.

### Won't lower-commitment offers just attract tyre-kickers?

Some leads from surface-level offers will not be sellers. That is expected. The qualifying question on the form and the follow-up conversation will separate genuine owners from curious neighbours. The value is in identifying the owners who care enough about their local market to raise their hand — even if they are not ready to list today.

### Should the same lead see ads from multiple levels?

Yes, and this happens naturally. A homeowner who downloads a sold-price list may later see a renovation guide or an appraisal ad. The ladder is not a linear path that each person walks in order — it is a set of entry points that Meta can match to the same person at different times.
