# How to win seller leads with a suburb sold-price list

A homeowner may want local evidence before they want an appraisal. Build a short, current list of disclosed sales for one suburb and one recognisable home type. The list is the offer; the ad introduces it.

This is an early-intent campaign, not a promise of listings. The 60–90 minute setup estimate starts only after you have confirmed data rights, image permissions, a privacy notice and the delivery path.

## At a glance

- Offer: recent disclosed sales for one suburb and home type.
- Example test: A$25–A$30 daily for 14 days (a planning example, not a benchmark).
- Primary measure: cost per valid, contactable homeowner.
- Prerequisites: source terms, image permissions, privacy notice and separate marketing consent.

## Why this offer is useful

In a 2024 realestate.com.au survey of sellers using online sources (n=1,023), 75% said they looked at sold-property listings when finding an agent. That is context for the offer, not evidence that this campaign will perform. See [realestate.com.au seller-leads guidance](https://customer.realestate.com.au/agent-resources/agency-marketplace/seller-leads-best-practice/).

## Build the list

1. Choose the patch where you want more listings and define a reporting period.
2. Choose a home type owners can recognise, such as single-storey detached homes.
3. Include the address, disclosed price, sale date, factual description, permitted image and last-updated date.
4. Use agency records or a provider whose terms allow republication. Portal visibility is not permission.
5. Leave withheld prices out and include a plain “not a valuation” note.

Download the [synthetic CSV template](/guides/resources/sold-price-list-seller-leads/synthetic-sold-price-list.csv). Its addresses and figures are fictional and must not be represented as sales.

## Campaign and geographic scope

For a first test, use one offer, one form and a named review date. Download the [lead ad copy](/guides/resources/sold-price-list-seller-leads/seller-ad-copy.txt). A$25–A$30 daily for 14 days is a planning range only. Check the current Meta account requirements for housing-related ads and your account country and markets. Meta Special Ad Category restrictions can apply when an ad is based in or reaches the US, Canada or Europe; Australian domestic campaigns are not universally subject to one radius rule. Nondiscrimination always applies.

## Form, qualification and consent

Ask for full name and email. Ask for mobile only when phone follow-up is explained and permitted. Use this question:

> Do you own a home of this type in [Suburb]? Yes / No / Prefer not to say

Delivering the requested list and adding ongoing marketing are separate actions:

> Optional: Yes, send me occasional [Suburb] property updates from [Agency]. This is separate from receiving the sold-price list. I can unsubscribe at any time.

Link the privacy policy and record the wording, timestamp, source, channels consented and later suppression. Download the [form and consent draft](/guides/resources/sold-price-list-seller-leads/seller-qualification-form.txt).

## Delivery and follow-up

Put the list on the thank-you screen and send a complete email immediately or within the same business day. Use the [delivery email draft](/guides/resources/sold-price-list-seller-leads/delivery-email.txt). Identify the agent, explain the scope and offer to explain a comparable; do not imply the person asked for an appraisal.

## Hypothetical review

Suppose spend is A$420, 28 form leads, 21 valid contacts, 12 homeowners, 6 useful seller conversations, 3 appraisals and 1 listing. The rates are 21/28=75.0%, 12/21=57.1%, 6/12=50.0%, 3/6=50.0% and 1/3=33.3%. Costs are A$15.00, A$20.00, A$35.00, A$70.00, A$140.00 and A$420.00 at each stage. These are arithmetic examples, not universal cut-offs or expected results.

Review broken links and delivery promptly. At the named review date, continue, revise or stop based on your own evidence and economics.

## FAQs

### Is a sold-price list the same as a property valuation?

No. It is recent, disclosed market evidence for a defined group of properties, not a valuation of one home.

### Should I use an instant form or a website form?

An instant form is a sensible first test for a fast mobile submission. A website form suits a flow that needs more explanation or an on-site action.

### How often should I update the list?

Choose a frequency you can maintain. Monthly may suit an active suburb; a slower market may need a longer interval. Show the last-updated date.
