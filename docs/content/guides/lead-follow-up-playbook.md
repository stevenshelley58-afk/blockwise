# A practical 14-day follow-up cadence for real estate leads

Follow up as a service, not a chase. Deliver what was requested, use only permitted channels, ask one useful question and record the person’s preference. A click or one form submission does not prove urgency.

Download the [14-day cadence CSV](/guides/resources/lead-follow-up-playbook/14-day-cadence.csv) and [message specimens](/guides/resources/lead-follow-up-playbook/message-specimens.txt).

## Day by day

The default for a silent, low-intent resource downloader is three touches: delivery email, one permitted check and one preferred-channel follow-up. An active request can change that cadence only after you agree the time and channel.

| Day | Channel | Condition | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Email | Resource requested; email permitted | Deliver it and identify the sender |
| 1 | No send | Review | Check delivery, replies, consent and preference |
| 2 | Call | Phone requested or permitted; allowed hour | One service-led check; do not add a call if it was not requested |
| 3–6 | No send | No reply | Wait; do not add another channel |
| 7 | Email or SMS | One preferred channel, with consent | One helpful follow-up with opt-out |
| 8–13 | No send | No reply | Wait and maintain suppression records |
| 14 | Review | All | Agree a next step with engaged people; otherwise pause |

Download the [14-day cadence CSV](/guides/resources/lead-follow-up-playbook/14-day-cadence.csv). Its no-send rows are intentional.

## Branches

- Reply: pause the generic cadence, answer the question and agree the next step.
- No answer: make only the next permitted attempt and then pause; do not escalate volume.
- Appointment: confirm time and purpose, then suppress generic reminders.
- Refusal: thank the person and mark the requested channels as do-not-contact.
- Unsubscribe: stop commercial electronic messages and update suppression promptly.

## Telephone compliance is separate

Before calling, check the [Do Not Call Register](https://www.donotcall.gov.au/) where relevant, have consent or another lawful basis, call only in allowed hours, show caller ID, identify yourself and the agency, and end immediately when asked. A service call is not an automatic exemption. See [ACMA: Say no to telemarketers](https://www.acma.gov.au/say-no-to-telemarketers). This is general guidance, not legal certification.

## Email and SMS compliance

Commercial email and SMS require consent unless a narrow exception applies. Identify the sender, provide a functional unsubscribe mechanism and honour an unsubscribe request within five working days. Keep evidence of wording, source, timestamp, channel and suppression. These rules are distinct from telephone obligations. See [ACMA: Avoid sending spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) and [OAIC: Direct marketing](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing).

After day 14, use an agreed, permission-based next step. Do not default to weekly email for a year. If there was no engagement, pause.

## FAQs

### How many times should I contact someone who does not answer?

Use the documented 14-day cadence only where the channel is permitted and the person has not opted out. There is no universal attempt count.

### Can I send SMS or email after someone requests a list?

Only where the request and consent record cover that message. Identify the sender, provide unsubscribe and honour requests within five working days.

### What is different about telephone follow-up?

Phone calls have separate obligations, including the Do Not Call Register where relevant, consent or another lawful basis, allowed hours, caller ID and immediate stop when asked.

### What should I do after a reply or appointment?

Pause the generic cadence, answer the question, record the preference and agree the next step. Suppress commercial messages when asked.
