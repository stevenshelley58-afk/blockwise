# A practical follow-up cadence for real estate leads

Generating leads is the easy part. The hard part — the part that determines whether you build a pipeline or waste money — is what happens after the form is submitted.

A documented cadence beats ad-hoc calling. When you have a written schedule of calls, emails and SMS touches, you do not rely on memory, and you do not let leads go cold because nobody remembered to follow up.

## Why most agents lose leads

Three failure patterns account for the majority of wasted lead spend.

**Pattern 1: No CRM.** Leads arrive in Ads Manager or an email inbox and sit there. Nobody is assigned, nobody is notified, and the lead goes cold before anyone calls. This is the most common pattern and the easiest to fix.

**Pattern 2: One call, then quit.** An agent calls a lead once, does not get an answer, and moves on. Most leads do not answer the first call — many online real estate leads are early in their search and not ready to talk. One call is not a follow-up strategy; it is a coincidence check.

**Pattern 3: No system.** The agent has a CRM but does not use it consistently. Leads are not categorised, cadences are not set, and there is no record of which leads were called, when, or what was said. Without a system, follow-up becomes a memory test, and memory is not a strategy.

## The cost of slow follow-up

Speed-to-lead matters — a lead contacted within the same business day is more likely to engage than one contacted three days later.

But the five-minute rule has become an excuse. Agents who cannot respond in five minutes conclude that lead generation does not work, when the real problem is that they have no follow-up system at all.

The practical standard is this: **call within 24 hours, then stay on a cadence.** You do not need to beat five minutes to build a pipeline. You need to call, keep calling on a schedule, and use your CRM to maintain the cadence over weeks and months.

## The follow-up cadence: three phases

This system assumes you have a CRM (KV Core, Follow Up Boss, Lofty, Real Geeks or similar) and can set up automated emails and SMS. The cadence has three phases:

| Phase | Period | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Response | Day 0 to 14 | Deliver the resource and establish contact |
| Qualification | Day 15 to 90 | Understand intent, timing and next action |
| Permission-based nurture | After day 90 | Continue useful updates while consent remains valid |

## Compliance: before you send any SMS or email

Before setting up any SMS or email automation, understand your obligations under Australian law.

**Australian commercial email and SMS rules** require consent, accurate sender identification and a functional unsubscribe mechanism. Unsubscribe requests must be honoured within five working days. Only send commercial messages to people who have consented or who fall within the limited exceptions in the Spam Act.

**Behavioural data** (such as listing views) should be used in a way consistent with the person's reasonable expectations and the agency's privacy notice. Do not use search or viewing history in ways the lead would not expect.

- **ACMA — Avoid sending spam:** https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam
- **OAIC — Direct marketing guidance:** https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing

## Phase 1: Response (Day 0 to 14)

Deliver the resource the lead requested and establish contact. The goal of this phase is to reach the lead, confirm they received what they asked for, and start a conversation — not to pitch.

| Day | Action | Channel | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Call within 24 hours of lead registration | Phone | First contact attempt; introduce yourself and confirm the lead received what they requested |
| 2 | Send a personalised email referencing the specific property or list they viewed | Email | Provide value without asking for a call |
| 3 | Call again | Phone | Second attempt |
| 5 | Send an SMS: "Hi [name], just checking you got the list. Any questions about specific properties?" | SMS | Low-friction re-engagement |
| 7 | Call again | Phone | Third attempt |
| 10 | Send a relevant local market update or new listing that matches their search | Email | Demonstrate ongoing value |
| 12 | Call again | Phone | Fourth attempt |
| 14 | Send an SMS offering a 15-minute call to discuss their search | SMS | Direct appointment attempt |

If you connect on any call during this phase, move the lead to a tailored cadence based on their timeline (see below). Do not continue the generic sequence after a conversation has happened.

## Phase 2: Qualification (Day 15 to 90)

If you have not connected after 14 days, shift to understanding the lead's intent, timing and next action. The goal is qualification, not volume — you are trying to learn whether this lead has a real timeline, not just to rack up call attempts.

- **Every 21 days:** One phone call
- **Weekly:** Automated email (market update, new listings, or a branded newsletter)
- **Ongoing:** Automated listing alerts matching their search criteria, set up in your CRM based on the properties they viewed

This phase continues to day 90. If the lead engages during this phase — answers a call, replies to an email, or clicks through on listing alerts — move them to a tailored cadence based on their timeline.

## Phase 3: Permission-based nurture (After day 90)

After day 90, the lead moves to long-term nurture. This phase continues **only while consent remains valid**. If the lead unsubscribes or asks you to stop, remove them from automated sequences immediately.

- **Monthly:** One phone call to leads who have not explicitly opted out
- **Weekly:** Automated email (market update, new listings)
- **Ongoing:** Automated listing alerts matching their search criteria
- **Review regularly:** Prune the sequence — remove leads who have not engaged in an extended period, and review whether the content is still relevant

The 21-day rotation from Phase 2 catches leads whose timeline shortens during this period. A lead who has been quiet for three months may click on a listing alert and become active again — that is your signal to call.

## The CRM automation layer

Phone calls are manual. Everything else should be automated in your CRM.

Set up an **automated nurture sequence** that continues while consent remains valid. Review and prune the sequence regularly — a three-year automated flow that no one has reviewed in three years is a compliance risk, not a strategy. The sequence should include:

- **Property alerts:** Automated listing matches based on the lead's search history, sent daily or as new listings hit the market
- **Weekly email:** A short, consistent touch point — a market update, a local tip, or a relevant new listing. Keep it short and useful, not a newsletter
- **Monthly market report:** Sent automatically to all active leads, showing recent sales and listing activity in their area
- **SMS touches:** Interspersed with the email cadence, but not more than once per week to avoid fatigue. Only send SMS to leads who have consented to receive them

The goal of automation is not to replace the phone call. It is to keep your name in front of the lead between calls so that when they are ready to talk, you are the agent they think of.

## The first call: what to say and what to ask

The first call has a clear sequence. Do not ask multiple questions at once — let the lead engage at their own pace.

1. **Confirm they received the requested resource.** Did they get the list, the property details, the link? This is a service call, not a sales call.

2. **Ask whether anything specific caught their attention.** If they have looked at the resource, what stood out? This tells you what they care about without asking them to commit to a position.

3. **Ask what they are trying to achieve.** What are they looking for? What area? Let them tell you — do not recite their search history back to them, even if you can see it. If they feel observed rather than heard, trust drops.

4. **Ask timing only after they engage.** When are they hoping to move? This is the single most important piece of information you will get, and it determines everything that follows. But ask it only once the lead is talking — not as the opening question.

### A script for the first call

> "Hi [name], it's [your name] from [agency]. You recently requested the [list/property details] — I just wanted to make sure you got that and see if you had any questions.
>
> [Pause. If they confirm, continue.]
>
> Was there anything in particular that caught your attention?
>
> [Listen. Note what they mention.]
>
> So I can point you in the right direction — what are you trying to achieve? Are you looking for yourself, or helping someone else?
>
> [Listen. Once they are engaged and talking, ask:]
>
> And what's your timeline? Are you just starting to look, or are you hoping to make a move in the next few months?
>
> [Listen. This determines your follow-up intensity.]"

Do not pitch on the first call unless they ask. The goal is information, not an appointment. Appointments come later, once you understand what they need and when they need it.

## Timeline-based follow-up intensity

After the first conversation, adjust your cadence to match their timeline.

| Timeline | Call frequency | Email frequency | Purpose | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0–30 days (hot)** | Once per week | Weekly + listing alerts | Set an appointment and get face-to-face | Lead books an appointment or asks you to stop |
| **30–90 days (warm)** | Once every two weeks | Weekly + listing alerts | Maintain contact, identify specific properties, move toward appointment | Lead books, unsubscribes, or explicitly asks you to stop |
| **3–6 months (nurture)** | Once every three weeks | Weekly market email + alerts | Stay top-of-mind until timeline shortens | Lead unsubscribes or asks you to stop |
| **6–12 months (pipeline)** | Once per month | Weekly market email + alerts | Gentle presence; do not let them forget you | Lead unsubscribes or asks you to stop |
| **12+ months (long-term)** | Once per quarter | Weekly market email + alerts | Maintain minimum contact until their timeline changes | Lead unsubscribes or asks you to stop |

When a lead's timeline shortens — they call you, reply to an email, or click through on a listing alert — move them up a tier immediately. A 6-month lead who starts clicking on listings every day is now a 30-day lead.

## The listing alert advantage

Automated listing alerts are the highest-leverage tool in your CRM.

When you set a lead up on listing alerts, they receive new properties matching their search criteria automatically. This does three things:

1. **It delivers ongoing value without manual effort.** The lead sees new listings without having to search, which keeps them engaged with your website rather than a portal.
2. **It generates intent signals.** Your CRM tracks which listings the lead clicks on, how many photos they view, and how often they return. A spike in activity is your signal to call.
3. **It creates a natural reason to follow up.** When a lead clicks on a specific property multiple times, you have a reason to call that is not a cold check-in: "You recently viewed this property through our site. Would you like the details or inspection times?"

Set listing alerts up for every lead, regardless of timeline. The alerts work in the background for months and produce calls when the lead is ready, not when you guess they might be.

## The Messenger option

Meta now supports starting a Messenger conversation after a lead submits a form. When enabled, the lead agrees to receive messages from your business, and a conversation opens in Messenger with their contact information pre-populated.

This is a second channel, not a replacement for calling. Use it for:

- A quick thank-you message after the form is submitted
- Sending a direct link to a specific property they asked about
- Following up when they do not answer the phone — some leads prefer text-based communication

Keep Messenger messages short and conversational. Do not copy your email cadence into Messenger — the medium is different and long messages get ignored.

## How to track follow-up in your CRM

Your CRM is not just a contact database. It is your follow-up scoreboard. Track these fields for every lead:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Lead source (campaign name) | So you know which ads produce closable leads |
| Date registered | So you know how long the lead has been in your system |
| Number of call attempts | So you know whether you are following the cadence |
| Last contact date | So you know when the next touch is due |
| Timeline (hot/warm/nurture/pipeline) | So your call frequency matches their intent |
| Property type / area of interest | So listing alerts are relevant |
| Search history (if available) | So you can call about specific properties they viewed |
| Status (new/attempting/contacted/appointment/closed/lost) | So you can see your pipeline at a glance |
| Consent status | So you know whether you can send SMS and email |
| Unsubscribe date | So you can ensure no further commercial messages are sent |

Review your CRM pipeline weekly. If you have 20 leads in "attempting" status and zero in "contacted," your call cadence is not working. If you have 50 leads in "contacted" and zero appointments, your conversation script needs work. The CRM tells you where the breakdown is — but only if you use it.

## Common follow-up mistakes

- **Calling once and giving up.** Most leads do not answer the first call. That is normal, not a signal to stop.
- **Not calling at all.** Relying solely on email and SMS automation produces a pipeline of leads who never hear a human voice. Deals happen on the phone.
- **Pitching on the first call.** The first call is for information, not a listing presentation. Leads who feel sold-to on the first call stop responding.
- **No cadence.** Random calling — whenever you "get around to it" — is not a system. Leads fall through the gaps and you cannot diagnose why.
- **Not using listing alerts.** This is the single biggest missed opportunity. Listing alerts do the nurturing work automatically and produce intent signals you can act on.
- **Treating leads as numbers.** Leads are people in one of the biggest financial decisions of their lives. Agents who treat them as entries in a spreadsheet do not build the trust needed to close.
- **Ignoring consent and unsubscribe requests.** Continuing to send SMS or email after a lead has unsubscribed is a breach of the Spam Act, not a follow-up strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many times should I call a lead before giving up?

Do not give up after a fixed number of calls. Follow the 14-day response cadence (roughly 4–5 call attempts), then move to a 21-day rotation through the qualification phase (day 15 to 90), then to a longer nurture cadence after day 90. A lead who has not engaged in an extended period should be reviewed — if they have unsubscribed, stop all commercial messages.

### Should I use SMS or email for automated follow-up?

Both, but only with consent. SMS has higher open rates but lower tolerance for frequency. Email allows longer content and listing alerts. Use SMS for short check-ins (no more than once per week) and email for property alerts, market updates and newsletters. Ensure every message includes a functional unsubscribe mechanism, and honour unsubscribe requests within five working days.

### What if I do not have a CRM?

Get one. Most brokerages provide a free CRM (KV Core at eXp, for example). If your brokerage does not, Follow Up Boss, Lofty and Real Geeks all offer affordable plans. Without a CRM, you cannot run a systematic follow-up cadence — you are relying on memory, which is not a strategy.

### Should I hire an ISA (inside sales agent) to handle calls?

If your lead volume exceeds what you can call personally within 24 hours, an ISA is the next step. The system described here works whether the caller is you or a trained ISA. The key is that someone calls within 24 hours and stays on the cadence.

### Does this work for both Facebook and Google leads?

Yes. The cadence is platform-agnostic. Google leads tend to be higher-intent (they actively searched for "homes for sale"), so they may engage faster. Facebook leads tend to be earlier in the process, so the nurture phase may run longer. The system handles both.

### What are my compliance obligations for SMS and email follow-up?

Australian commercial email and SMS rules require consent, accurate sender identification and a functional unsubscribe mechanism. Unsubscribe requests must be honoured within five working days. Only send commercial messages to people who have consented or who fall within the limited exceptions in the Spam Act. See the [ACMA guidance on avoiding spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam) and the [OAIC direct marketing guidance](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/direct-marketing).
